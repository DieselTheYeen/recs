The Covenant Code

I. Growth is mandatory, stagnation is failure. Every member is expected to pursue improvement, whether through combat, skill, knowledge, discipline or self-reflection; remaining unchanged is a choice the Covenant does not respect. II. Failure is not a crime, but refusing to learn from it is. Members may fail, lose spars, make mistakes and fall short of expectations, but what matters is how they respond afterwards — excuses are worthless, growth is not. III. Challenges must be honoured. When challenged by a superior, mentor, member or sanctioned trial, members are expected to participate unless given legitimate reason not to. The Covenant was built upon testing oneself, where avoidance breeds weakness. IV. Respect is earned, not demanded; rank grants authority, yet it does not automatically grant admiration. Members are expected to respect the position of their superiors while understanding that true respect is built through action, consistency and example. V. Nobody suffers alone — the Covenant does not coddle weakness, neither does it ignore. Everyone is expected to aid one another in growth and survival; struggle may be personal, but improvement is a collective responsibility. VI. Strength serves purpose, because power without discipline is a liability. Those who abuse their strength, status or authority for personal gratification at the expense of the Covenant's stability will answer for it. VII. There will always be truth over comfort, for dishonesty weakens the foundation of trust. Members are expected to speak honestly, accept criticism and confront reality rather than hide behind pride, excuses or self-deception. VIII. The Covenant comes before ego, where rivalries are encouraged, but sabotage is not. Competition should strengthen the kingdom, not divide it — personal grudges must never threaten the pack's stability. IX. Victories and failures alike are lessons, those who reside are expected to carry their experiences with pride rather than shame, for The Covenant values perseverance more than perfection. X. Earn your place; no rank is permanent, no achievement lasts forever, and every member, from initiate to sovereign, is expected to continue proving their worth through action. Yesterday's accomplishments do not excuse today's complacency.

The Final Law

You are not judged for what you were, you are judged for what you choose to become. This single rule embodies the entire Covenant's philosophy.
